01From cassettes to AI

Music companies once invested heavily before a song reached anyone, and listeners bought cassettes, CDs (Compact Discs) and DVDs (Digital Versatile Discs). Smartphones, internet and YouTube changed that: every artist can now upload directly. Today an artist has to be the creator, investor, technician and distributor at once — and AI has added both a new helper and new risks.

Then
Labels controlled the market

Companies chose which songs got recorded, pressed and sold. Radio and TV were the only way to be heard.

Now
Global from day one

One upload reaches Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube and TikTok, and the Nepali diaspora in the Gulf, UK, US and Australia. You keep ownership of your master recording.

Next
The AI era

AI makes demos, designs and captions faster — but it also makes voice cloning and fake uploads easy. Knowing both sides is now part of the job.

02Secure your accounts before you post

Losing a page with lakhs of followers because a recovery number was never updated is the mistake that actually costs artists their career. Do this before you upload anything.

01
Keep your recovery email and phone number current

On Facebook: Settings & Privacy → Settings → Accounts Center → your profile → Personal Details → Contact Information. Add the email and number you control today and remove old ones. An inaccessible recovery email is like someone else holding your house key.

02
Turn on Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) on Facebook

A password alone can leak through phishing, guessing or a virus. With 2FA, a login also needs a one-time code. Go to Accounts Center → Password and security → Two-factor authentication, choose your profile, then pick an authenticator app (most secure), SMS (Short Message Service) or WhatsApp (easiest), or a security key. Enter the 6-digit code to confirm.

03
Turn on 2-Step Verification on Gmail / Google

Your YouTube channel lives inside your Google account. Go to Manage your Google Account → Security → 2-Step Verification → Turn on, confirm your password, add your number with the +977 code, choose text message and enter the code.

04
Save your backup codes somewhere safe

Both Facebook and Google give one-time backup codes. Write them in a notebook or a second email account — if your SIM is lost or blocked, they are the only way back in.

03Distribution & metadata

Nepal has no local front door onto Spotify or Apple Music — independent artists use a distributor (aggregator). One submission reaches all the major stores and social platforms.

Uploading to a distributor
01
Release details

Choose Single or Album and link your official artist profile.

02
Choose stores

Tick Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, JioSaavn, Amazon and the rest.

03
Cover and audio

Cover art 3000 × 3000 pixels, square. Audio as a high-quality WAV (Waveform Audio File), ideally 24-bit.

04
Metadata and copyright

Enter the real legal names of lyricist and composer, and tick Content ID and copyright options.

Metadata rules that decide whether you are found
Artist name
Never change it

Right: "Rajendra Aryal". Wrong: "Me", "Unknown" or a different spelling on each release — Spotify will create a separate profile.

Song title
Short and clean

Right: "सङ्गीतमा सफलता". Wrong: "sangit ma safalta" or "(prod. xyz)" in the title — producer credits go in the credits section.

Settings
Genre, date, language

Pick the correct genre (for example Folk), write the date as YYYY-MM-DD, and set the language that more than 50% of the song is sung in.

Duration
Match the audio

The length you enter must match the real audio file, or stores will reject the release.

International codes

Get found: Google SEO and AEO
SEO (Search Engine Optimization)
Show up in Google and YouTube search

Title: song name + singer + "New Nepali Song 2026", in Nepali and Roman script. Put singer, lyricist and composer in the first two lines of the description. Use a custom thumbnail, set the language to Nepali, add lyrics as subtitles, and use only 3–5 hashtags. Tags play only a small role — don't stuff keywords.

AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)
Show up in AI answers

ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews build answers from public information. Use the same name and spelling everywhere, add a short question-and-answer line to descriptions ("Who sang this song? — …"), keep full credits and a bio on an official website, and verify your Official Artist Channel and Spotify for Artists.

05Copyright & royalties

Distribution puts your song online. Registration and the right settings make sure it stays yours — and pays you.

Master copyright
The recording

Your voice and that specific production. You own it if you paid for the recording or are the producer. Earns from streaming and downloads.

Publishing copyright
The composition

The lyrics and melody. You own it if you wrote the song or composed the tune. Earns from radio, TV, public performance and concerts — even when AI copies the voice, the song stays yours.

Register
Copyright Registrar's Office

Register online at nepalcopyright.gov.np. It creates a dated, official record of authorship — your strongest evidence in any dispute. Check the current fee on the site.

Collect
MRCSN (Music Royalty Collection Society Nepal)

Nepal's official royalty collection society for lyricists, composers and publishers. Linked with CISAC and WIPO, so registered songs can also collect royalties from abroad, paid quarterly to your Nepali bank account.

Claim or strike?
Content ID claim
Automatic, and it pays you

YouTube scans uploads for your music. When someone uses your song, the claim sends that video's ad revenue to you. The uploader's channel is not harmed.

Copyright strike
Manual, and nobody earns

A takedown request removes the video and puts a strike on the channel. Use it only when someone steals your full song or video.

Fan covers
Don't block them

Let fan covers and dance videos run with Content ID on — free promotion plus income. A misleading AI voice clone is different and can be reported.

06Uploading to Facebook & YouTube

Distribution gets your song onto streaming platforms, but reels and music videos still need a direct upload.

Facebook
Bulk upload via Meta Business Suite

1. Open Meta Business Suite and go to Home.
2. Click More → Bulk upload reels (up to 50 videos at once).
3. Add your videos — each gets an automatic copyright check.
4. Write the caption and hashtags, choose a thumbnail, set language to Nepali.
5. Under Optimize, turn on captions, Stars and Content Monetization if eligible.
6. Under Publish, Share now or Schedule (Asia/Kathmandu time).

YouTube
Upload via YouTube Studio

1. Open YouTube Studio → Create → Upload videos.
2. Add the title and full description with credits.
3. Under Video elements, add subtitles, an end screen and cards.
4. Wait for the copyright Checks — results can still change later.
5. Keep it Private or Unlisted until HD (High Definition) processing finishes, then go Public or Premiere.
6. Set audience ("not made for kids" unless it is), AI use disclosure, tags and video language.

Thumbnail
Make your own

Don't let a random frame become the thumbnail. Use a separate, high-contrast 1280 × 720 image.

Shorts
Vertical, up to 3 minutes

9:16 vertical clips up to 3 minutes count as Shorts. #Shorts is optional. Use Add Sound to link your official track so viewers find the full song.

AI disclosure
Say "Yes" when it applies

If AI made a realistic voice, person or scene, YouTube requires you to disclose it. Hiding it can lead to action on the video.

07Earning & getting paid

Streaming alone rarely pays the bills. Rough workshop estimates for 1 million streams: about $200–500 from Nepal, India or Bangladesh audiences, and several thousand dollars from the US, UK, Australia or Canada. Real numbers vary a lot by channel, genre and season.

Ads
Monetization

YPP (YouTube Partner Program) and Facebook in-stream ads. Videos over 8 minutes can carry mid-roll ads.

Brands
Sponsorship

Product placement in music videos and reels.

Fans
Direct support

Memberships, live gifts, Patreon or Facebook subscriptions, and direct sales on sites like Bandcamp.

Stage
Live concerts

The more a song travels online, the more your stage and concert value grows.

Evergreen
Everyday music

Bhajans, birthday songs, lullabies, meditation and lofi (low-fidelity) music are played daily all year — steady, long-term income.

Promotion
Ads that reach Nepalis

Skip the Boost button — use Meta Ads Manager, target Australia, USA, UK and UAE with language set to Nepali, and promote your best 15-second hook. On YouTube, In-Feed video ads bring real watch time.

Getting paid, from Nepal
01
A payout method that reaches Nepal

PayPal cannot send funds into Nepal, so most Nepali creators use Payoneer or a bank that accepts inward SWIFT (international bank) transfers.

02
Bring money in through a recognised channel

Nepal Rastra Bank regulates inward remittance. Use a licensed service or your bank's foreign currency account to keep a paper trail.

03
Pay for ads with a prepaid dollar card

Take your PAN (Permanent Account Number) card and citizenship to any commercial bank and request a prepaid dollar card linked to your account. The yearly limit follows Nepal Rastra Bank rules — ask your bank.

04
Treat it as taxable income

Streaming and royalty income is generally business or self-employment income. Register a PAN with the Inland Revenue Department, keep records of every payout, and talk to a chartered accountant early.

Not tax adviceRates, thresholds and forms change. Confirm with a Nepali chartered accountant or the Inland Revenue Department before you file.
08AI: opportunity, risk & smart use

AI is a tool — the creative direction is the artist's. Humming the tune, writing the words and choosing the feeling stay human; AI speeds up the repetitive technical work.

50%+

of new daily uploads on Deezer were fully AI-generated in June 2026 — about 90,000 tracks a day.

1–3%

of total streams went to AI music (April 2026). Listeners still choose human artists.

What is happening in Nepal
June 2026
Sugam Pokharel

Publicly warned against unauthorized AI covers of his songs — while using Suno himself for arrangement sketches. The problem is use without permission, not AI itself.

June 2026
Sunil Giri

The singer and composer personally removed more than 50 unauthorized AI-generated uploads from YouTube.

Label case
Bhavna Music Solution

40–50 songs were altered with AI and sold under other names; one person ran 10–12 channels. Complaints against about 95 channels reached the Nepal Police Cyber Bureau.

The biggest risk
Fake strikes on real artists

If an AI channel registers a cloned song first, automatic systems can flag the original artist. Register rights through your distributor before release and keep dated proof of creation.

Using AI in Nepali music, without depending on it
Making
Human first, AI assists

Feeling and story: you. Lyrics and melody: yours, with AI suggesting rhymes. Demo sketch: AI. Real vocals and madal, sarangi, bansuri: human musicians. Mix and master: engineer with AI tools. Final decision: you.

Cover art
AI for ideas, human for design

Write a brief, get 5–10 concepts from AI with "no text" in the prompt, pick one, use a real photo of the artist, then finish in Photoshop or Canva and type the Nepali title yourself — AI often breaks Devanagari letters. Check 3000 × 3000 px and no logos, links or phone numbers.

Thumbnails
Real faces, honest scenes

1280 × 720, under 2 MB, a big Nepali title in 3–4 words. Let AI clean or extend the background and fix light, then test versions with YouTube Test & Compare. Never show scenes that aren't in the video or AI faces of other celebrities.

Simple rule Whatever makes people recognise you, do it yourself. Give AI the drafts — rhymes, demo sketches, moodboards, caption ideas, translations — and keep your voice, your instruments, your final design and your relationship with fans in your own hands.
Protect yourself in the AI era
01
Ask five questions before uploading to any AI tool

Will my file train their model, and can I opt out? Who owns the output, and is the free plan different? How long is it kept, and can I delete it? Which country's law applies? Do I have permission for any other person's voice or work in it? If you can't answer, don't upload unreleased masters or raw vocals.

02
Add AI clauses to your contracts

Written consent and separate payment for any voice cloning, a ban on using masters, stems or lyrics for AI training, disclosure of where AI was used, and a share of income from AI versions.

03
If your song is stolen

Save evidence (links, screenshots, dates, views). Report on the platform, or dispute a false claim. Tell your distributor. For fraud or commercial misuse, complain to the Nepal Police Cyber Bureau, and consult a lawyer or the Copyright Registrar's Office.

The laws around you
Nepal
Existing laws, no AI law yet

Copyright Act 2059 protects songs, melodies, lyrics and recordings but was not written with voice cloning in mind. The Privacy Act 2075 and Electronic Transactions Act 2063 can help. The National AI Policy 2082 (August 2025) is a policy, not a law.

Europe
EU (European Union) AI Act

From 2 August 2026, deepfakes — AI-made or altered voices, images and video — must be clearly labelled. This can affect content reaching the diaspora in Europe.

USA
ELVIS Act and copyright

Tennessee's 2024 ELVIS Act protects artists' voices from AI imitation. In the US, copyright needs human contribution.

Industry
Labels, IFPI and RIAA

IFPI (International Federation of the Phonographic Industry) represents record companies worldwide and runs the ISRC system; RIAA (Recording Industry Association of America) represents US labels. Their voluntary labels: "Created by AI" and "AI-Assisted".

Not legal adviceLawsuits, policies and prices change quickly. Figures are as of September 2026. For a specific case, talk to a lawyer.
09AI for your own life

AI isn't only for music. Start with simple tasks, and move to daily and advanced work as you get comfortable.

General use
Understand and write

Explain a topic in simple words, help write emails and letters, translate Nepali ↔ English, read a form from a photo.

Daily use
Plan your day

To-do lists and schedules, household budget, meal, trip or event plans, help explaining children's homework.

Advanced use
Work smarter

Excel formulas and reports, summaries of long PDFs or contracts, a small website or app, automating repetitive tasks.

Four rules for safe use Ask clearly — say what you need, for whom and how long. Double-check names, numbers, laws and dates. Never share passwords, OTPs (One-Time Passwords), bank details or citizenship numbers. Keep AI as your assistant, not your boss.
